# Code of Conduct

## The point of this document

Moonfield asks people to learn in public: to write down a prediction, be wrong,
and then discuss why. That only works if being wrong here is safe.

Everything below follows from that.

## Expected behaviour

- **Assume good faith and genuine ignorance.** Someone asking what a timezone
  is has not failed a test; they are at the start of the curriculum.
- **Answer the question that was asked**, then optionally more.
- **Say "I don't know."** It is a normal and useful contribution, and it makes
  it safe for others to say it too.
- **Correct kindly and specifically.** "The longitude sign convention here is
  east-positive, so New York is −74" beats "wrong".
- **Share your errors.** A well-documented mistake teaches more than a success.
- **Remember the global audience.** Not everyone shares your hemisphere,
  season, coastline, calendar, first language, or level of prior schooling.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks
- Discriminatory language or imagery of any kind
- Sexual language or imagery
- Publishing others' private information
- Mocking a question for being basic — this one is taken seriously here
- Sustained disruption of discussion
- Deliberately posting incorrect astronomy as a joke

## Scope

Applies in all project spaces — issues, discussions, pull requests, commits,
code comments — and when representing the project elsewhere.

## Reporting

Report to the maintainers by opening a confidential report through GitHub's
private reporting, or by contacting a maintainer directly. Reports are handled
confidentially and reviewed as quickly as we can.

Do not report through a public issue.

## Enforcement

Maintainers will use judgement proportionate to the incident:

1. **Correction** — a private note explaining what was wrong
2. **Warning** — with a defined period of no interaction with those involved
3. **Temporary ban**
4. **Permanent ban**

Most incidents stop at step 1. That is the intent.
